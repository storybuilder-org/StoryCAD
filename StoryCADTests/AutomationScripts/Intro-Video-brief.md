# Intro video brief

The brief for `Intro-Video.scs` (#1421 Milestone 5, video design section 5). Approved by Terry, 2026-10-14. Change the brief first, then the script.

| # | Action on screen | Narration |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Title card: logo, "A Five-Minute Introduction to StoryCAD", music | (none) |
| 2 | Text card: "An outline is a plan of your story." | StoryCAD is a free and open source computer-aided design tool for fiction writers. It helps you build an outline: a working plan of your story that you develop before and during the draft, where an idea costs nothing to try or to discard. |
| 3 | Text card: "Story elements: Story Overview, Problem, Character, Setting, Scene" | An outline in StoryCAD is made of story elements. The story overview describes the work as a whole. Problems are the conflicts that drive the plot. Characters, settings and scenes are what you would expect, and a scene ties together a setting, a cast and an event. |
| 4 | StoryCAD opens; open the sample "The Old Man and the Sea" | Each outline is a single file with the extension dot S-T-B-X, which you can copy, move or back up like any other document. StoryCAD ships with sample outlines; this one is The Old Man and the Sea. |
| 5 | Stay on the outline | The navigation pane on the left shows the outline as a tree. Each node is a story element, with the story overview at the top and everything else beneath it. |
| 6 | Open Santiago | Selecting a node opens that element in the content pane on the right. Santiago is a character, so his page is a character form. |
| 7 | Click two of Santiago's tabs | Larger forms are divided into tabs, each covering one aspect of the element: for a character, role, appearance, backstory and more. Every major form also has a Notes tab. |
| 8 | Open Preferences | The menu bar across the top opens and saves outlines, adds and removes elements, runs the tools and prints reports. |
| 9 | Show Preferences, then Cancel | Preferences hold your name, your default outline folder and your backup and autosave settings. Your name becomes the author of each new outline. |
| 10 | New outline, blank template, named "Fish Story" | New outlines start from a template. This one uses the blank template, and the project name, Fish Story, becomes the file name. |
| 11 | Stay on the new outline | A blank outline has two nodes: the story overview, named for the story, and the trash can, which holds deleted elements until you restore or empty them. |
| 12 | Type the story idea | Work usually begins on the overview. The Story Idea tab records the idea that started it all. |
| 13 | Concept tab; type the concept | The Concept tab turns that idea into a question with conflict built in. "What if" is a good way in. |
| 14 | Add a problem, then Santiago and Manolin | You add story elements from the menu bar or by right-clicking a node in the tree. Here, a problem, the struggle that drives the story, and two characters, Santiago and Manolin. |
| 15 | Stay on the tree | There is no required order. Add elements as they occur to you, or work from beginning to end. Each one waits in the tree until you return to it. |
| 16 | Master Plots, "Ascent/Descent", Copy | StoryCAD's plotting aids are on the Tools menu. Master Plots offers patterns that stories have used for centuries, with notes on each. Copying Ascent/Descent adds its structure and suggested scenes to the outline. |
| 17 | Print Reports: check two report types, then Cancel | When you are ready to draft, print reports on any part of the outline. |
| 18 | Open the Reports menu so Scrivener Reports shows, then close it | If you draft in Scrivener, StoryCAD can export the outline into a Scrivener project, one document per story element. |
| 19 | File > Exit, don't save | StoryCAD is free from the Microsoft Store and the Mac App Store. Thanks for watching. |
| 20 | Closing card: "Like and subscribe" | (none) |
