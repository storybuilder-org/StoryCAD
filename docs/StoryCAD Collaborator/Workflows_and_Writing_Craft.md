# Workflows and Writing Craft

## What a workflow is

Collaborator divides the work of developing an outline into workflows, in the same sense the [Workflow](../Writing%20with%20StoryCAD/Workflow.html) page of this manual uses the word: a larger job broken into steps, each small enough to do and to judge on its own. A Collaborator workflow is one such step. You choose it from the list, Collaborator reads the story elements that step needs, and it proposes text for particular fields on those elements. Nothing is written until you accept it, so the workflow's job is to give you something concrete to react to, and yours is to decide.

Keeping each workflow to one craft question is deliberate. A suggestion for one Problem's goal and motivation can be weighed against what you know of the story; a suggestion that rewrote half the outline at once could not be. The [Workflow Reference](Workflows/) describes each workflow in turn.

## Craft questions and the workflows that answer them

The Writing with StoryCAD section of this manual teaches outlining as a sequence: idea and premise, then the story problem, then the characters who carry it, then structure, then scenes. Collaborator's workflows follow the same sequence, and each one has a page in that section that explains the craft behind it.

| Craft question | Read first | Workflow |
|----------------|------------|----------|
| What is my idea, concept, or premise? | [Story Idea, Concept, and Premise](../Writing%20with%20StoryCAD/Story_Idea_Concept_and_Premise.html) | Premise; Story Form for genre and story type |
| What is the main story problem? | [Defining Problems](../Writing%20with%20StoryCAD/Defining_Problems.html), [Problem and Character Development](../Writing%20with%20StoryCAD/Problem_and_Character_Development.html) | Story Problem; Problem Builder |
| What does my protagonist want, and what do they need? | [Defining Problems](../Writing%20with%20StoryCAD/Defining_Problems.html) | Inner and Outer Problems |
| How is the story shaped? | [Plotting with StoryCAD](../Writing%20with%20StoryCAD/Plotting_with_StoryCAD.html), [Structure Tab](../Story%20Elements/Structure_Tab.html) | Problem Builder, which chooses the beat sheet |
| Who is this character, and what are they for? | [Defining Characters](../Writing%20with%20StoryCAD/Defining_Characters.html) | Character Story Function; Define Character; Flaw and Backstory; Character Relationship |
| Where and when does it happen? | [The Importance of Setting](../Writing%20with%20StoryCAD/The_Importance_of_Setting.html), [Worldbuilding Guide](../Writing%20with%20StoryCAD/Worldbuilding_Guide.html) | Setting Builder; Define Story World |

A workflow's name in the list is sometimes longer than the name used here. Premise, for example, appears as Ideation (Story idea => Concept => Premise). The reference pages give both forms.

## The order teachers use

Writing classes usually teach story structure in roughly this order: idea, premise, story problem, then the goal, motivation, and conflict on each side of that problem, then characters, structure, and scenes. Collaborator walks the same order with fewer stops, because Problem Builder does the goal-motivation-conflict work and the structure work in one run, and Scene Builder does the scene work. You are free to run any workflow at any time. Running Scene Builder on an outline with an empty Overview will work, but the suggestions will be thin, because there is little for them to draw on; the craft has to come first, and then the tool has something to help with. [A Path to Try](Tutorial/A_Path_to_Try.html) sets out an order that wastes the least time.

For craft reading beyond this manual, see [StoryBuilder Resources](https://storybuilder.org/resources/) and [Learn](https://storybuilder.org/learn/).

## Workflows write to the forms you already use

StoryCAD stores your outline as forms with tabs and fields, and Collaborator suggests text for those same fields: the Premise on the Overview, a Goal on a Problem's Protagonist tab, the Flaw on a Character. When you accept a suggestion, open the same element in StoryCAD and the text is there, where you can edit it like anything else you typed.

An empty field is easy to try a suggestion on. A field that already holds your words is treated differently: Collaborator marks it Has your text and will not overwrite it without your deliberate review. [Reviewing Suggestions](Reviewing_Suggestions.html) explains the marks, and what Accept all changes does and does not touch.
