# Character Workflows

These workflows work on one Character at a time, and each opens a picker so you can say which. [Defining Characters](../../Writing%20with%20StoryCAD/Defining_Characters.html) is the craft behind them, and the [Character Form](../../Story%20Elements/Character_Form.html) pages describe the tabs they write. They divide the character between them: Character Story Function says what the character is for in the story, Define Character says who they are in the world, Flaw and Backstory says what is wrong with them and why, and Character Relationship says how they stand with one other person.

## Character Story Function

A character's place in the story is a different question from who they are. The [Role Tab](../../Story%20Elements/Role_Tab.html) page describes the Story Role field, which says how much of the story rests on the character (Major, Supporting, Minor, or Service), and the Archetype field, which names the pattern they play out: Hero, Mentor, Threshold Guardian, and the rest of Joseph Campbell's list.

Character Story Function proposes both, then writes a Character Sketch: a short description of the character's function in the story, drawn from those two choices, the Problems the character is linked to, the Flaw if there is one, and the story's premise. It is not a physical description, and it does not set the occupation Role; Define Character handles both of those. It is one of the five starred by default, because a character with a story role is a character the plot can use, and it is a sensible first workflow to run on any new character.

## Define Character

The Character form has tabs for who a person is in the world: Role for occupation, age, and sex; Physical and Appearance for how they look; Social for background; Psychological for how they think; and Inner Traits and Outer Traits for temperament and habit. Filling them one field at a time is slow, and the fields have to agree with each other, because a character's education, values, and manner come from one life. Define Character fills that sheet in one pass and keeps the parts consistent with each other and with any Problem where the character is protagonist or antagonist.

Pick the Character. The run proposes the Role (the character's occupation, as the Role Tab page describes it), Age, and Sex; Eyes, Hair, Build, Complexion, and the Appearance note; the four Social fields (Economic Status, Education, Ethnic and Cultural, and Religion); the Psychological fields (Personality Type, Intelligence, Values, Abnormality, and Focus); the twelve Inner Traits; and a list of Outer Traits. That is a long Property Updates list, and Review Each is the comfortable way through it. Define Character does not set the Story Role, the Character Sketch, the Flaw, or the Backstory; those belong to the workflows on either side of this one.

## Flaw and Backstory

The [Flaw Tab](../../Story%20Elements/Flaw_Tab.html) page calls a major flaw the root of a character arc: the thing that must be corrected, or will bring the character down. The [Backstory Tab](../../Story%20Elements/Backstory_Tab.html) page describes backstory as the history that explains a character's traits and motives, and warns against shoehorning explanations into it. The two belong together, since a flaw without a history is only a label, and this workflow writes them as a pair.

Pick the Character. If both fields are empty, the run proposes a flaw and a focused history that grounds it. If you have already written either, it keeps your facts and weaves the wound into them rather than replacing them; a filled field shows as Has your text and waits for your review. The Problems the character is linked to set the stakes, and where one of them is Person vs. Himself the run leans on it. Inner and Outer Problems can also write the Flaw from the problem side, and the last accept wins. This workflow does not touch the Story Role, the Character Sketch, the sheet fields Define Character writes, or Relationships.

## Character Relationship

The [Relationships Tab](../../Story%20Elements/Relationships_Tab.html) page describes a relationship as a dynamic between two people, distinct from either of them as an individual, and notes that the two sides of it seldom match. This workflow drafts that dynamic. It asks for two characters in turn, the Character and the Partner, and it works best when each has some sheet fill from the workflows above, though it runs on thin sheets too. It uses the traits you have filled and does not invent the ones you have not.

Accepting writes a relationship entry on both characters' Relationships tabs: the relationship type, the field the tab labels "is a," together with a Trait, an Attitude, and Notes. The Relationships Tab page explains each of those fields and how to edit the entry afterward.
