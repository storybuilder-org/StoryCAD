# An Example Session

This walkthrough is short enough for a class period or a first evening with Collaborator. Follow it in your own outline; the pictures come from a worked example.

That example is **Macbeth**, outlined in StoryCAD as if you were planning it yourself: a Story Idea on the Overview, a cast, a handful of settings, and five problems including the one that drives the play, The Crown Temptation.

It is deliberately not an empty outline. Most of what you meet in practice has some text in it already, and that is the more interesting case: Collaborator will not overwrite your words without asking, so a half-filled outline is where you learn what the controls really do. If your own outline is thinner, expect more proposals to apply straight away and fewer to ask for confirmation.

<!-- image: Macbeth Overview in StoryCAD with the Story Idea filled in -->

## 1. Start

1. Open StoryCAD and open your outline.  
2. On **Story Overview**, make sure there is at least a short **Story Idea**.  
3. Click **Collaborator** on the toolbar.

Collaborator reads what is there. Empty forms produce empty-sounding help.

## 2. Premise

1. Select the **Premise** workflow. The list shows it as a longer ideation label.  
2. Let it run.  
3. Read the header above the list. In the example it says **3: 0 free, 3 need review**, and the chat says the same thing in words.

All three proposals here are marked **Has your text**, because Macbeth's Concept, Description, and Premise were already written. **Accept all changes** would leave every one of them alone, which is the point: nothing you wrote disappears because you clicked the big blue button.

![Property Updates after the Premise workflow, showing three proposals that all need review](../../media/Collaborator-Property-Updates.png)

4. Use **Review Each** and read one of them properly. Compare **Yours** against **Proposed**, then Accept or Skip.

Return to StoryCAD and open the Overview. Anything you accepted is already there.

**Craft check:** Does the premise name a protagonist, a goal, and opposition? Macbeth's does: a noble who believes prophecy has made him king, the murder that secures it, and the conscience and resistance that turn the crown into a curse. If yours does not, edit it yourself or run **Try Again**. See [Story Idea, Concept, and Premise](../../Writing%20with%20StoryCAD/Story_Idea_Concept_and_Premise.html).

## 3. Story Problem

1. Run **Story Problem**.  
2. When Collaborator needs a character it opens a picker. Choose an existing one, or **Create a new element** if the person you want is not there yet.  
3. Accept the fields you want. Skip anything that feels wrong.

You should end with a main problem linked as the story problem, carrying a protagonist and an antagonist. In the example that is The Crown Temptation.

<!-- image: Problem form in StoryCAD after accepting Story Problem suggestions -->

## 4. GMC

1. Run **Goal / Motivation / Conflict (GMC)** on that problem.  
2. Note which rows say **New** or **Refresh** and which say **Has your text**.  
3. Use **Review Each** on at least one **Has your text** row. Read both versions. Accept or Skip deliberately.  
4. Use **Accept Free Remaining** for the rest.

<!-- image: Review Each showing Yours against Proposed for one GMC field -->

**Craft check:** Can you state what the protagonist wants, why, and what opposes them? Same for the antagonist? Macbeth wants the throne, because prophecy told him it is his, and what opposes him is Duncan's rightful claim and later his own conscience. See [Defining Problems](../../Writing%20with%20StoryCAD/Defining_Problems.html).

## 5. Optional next beat

If you have time, run one more. **Scene Conflict** on a scene is a good second look, and it behaves differently from the Overview workflows: in the example it proposed an Opposition the scene did not have, alongside an Outcome and a ProtagGoal that did. **Character Story Function** or **Flaw and Backstory** on the protagonist work just as well.

One more workflow is enough for a first session.

## 6. Close the loop

1. **Exit** Collaborator. Everything you accepted is already in your outline.  
2. In StoryCAD, open the Problem and Character elements and confirm the accepted text is there.

That is a full loop: workflow, suggestions, your judgment, outline updated.

## What you should notice

- You stayed in charge. Every replacement was one you approved.  
- Empty fields fill in bulk; fields with your words in them stop and ask.  
- One workflow equals one craft question, not "write my book."  
- Chat is optional. Nothing above required it.
